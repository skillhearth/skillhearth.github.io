Free: resume-bullet-rewriter

Free · MIT license

Paste your resume bullets and get each one rewritten side by side (before/after). It flags vague, repeated and weak-verb bullets and uses only facts you provide. Where a fact is missing it inserts an [ADD] marker and asks one short question. It never invents employers, numbers, tools or results, and it makes no promises about interviews or jobs.

Before / after

Fictional examples from our own test cases. The person, employer and bullets are invented; no real user is shown. Abridged output of the free skill.

Example 1: a duty, no data

Before: Responsible for answering customer emails

After: Answered customer emails

Stronger if you add data: Answered [ADD: number] customer emails per [ADD: day/week] and resolved [ADD: type of requests]

Flagged: WEAK VERB, NO RESULT (“responsible for” says role, not action)

It asks: Roughly how many emails did you answer per day or week?

No number was invented: the missing facts stay as markers until you supply them.

Example 2: a figure you gave

Before: Worked on improving the filing system. Reduced search time from 10 minutes to 3 minutes.

After: Improved the filing system, reducing document search time from 10 minutes to 3 minutes

Flagged: WEAK VERB (fixed); the figures are kept exactly as given

It asks: What did you change in the filing system?

The only numbers in the rewrite are the ones in the input.

Another test case: when asked to write “increased sales by 40%” with no data behind it, the skill did not write the figure. It returned “No usable line yet” and asked what you did that you think improved sales. It never promises interviews or jobs, and you must check that every line is true.

debt-payoff-budget-planner

US$9
Educational use only. Not financial, tax or legal advice. Simplified interest model (annual rate ÷ 12, no fees). It recommends no financial product or provider.

Enter your income, expenses and debts. It computes your monthly leftover and compares avalanche and snowball payoff, showing the arithmetic, using only your numbers.

  • Shows its assumptions: fixed rates, annual rate ÷ 12 compounded monthly, no fees, constant income and expenses. Real terms can differ.
  • Won't guess rates or fees you didn't give it, and won't say which method you should pick.
  • If your budget has no leftover, it shows the shortfall instead of a misleading projection.

Personal-use license, educational only.
